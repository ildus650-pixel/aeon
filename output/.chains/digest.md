1. *Anthropic's amended S-1 reveals $104B net loss on $33.24B revenue*
   Anthropic's amended S-1/A filing with the SEC reveals a $104B net loss for the first nine months of 2026, driven primarily by a $94B fair-value change on convertible notes and derivative liabilities. Revenue reached $33.24B across the period, up roughly 1,200% year-over-year, alongside $895.4B in future compute-and-infrastructure commitments.
   Why it matters: The filing quantifies how much of Anthropic's loss is accounting-driven versus operational, reshaping investor expectations ahead of its IPO.

2. *Binance $5.8B net outflow after Lazarus-linked hack*
   The FBI attributed the October 29 $6.3B Binance crypto heist to the Lazarus-linked TraderTraitor group, and the exchange processed approximately $5.8B in net outflows across Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain, and the XRP Ledger over the following 24 hours. About 95% of stolen assets have been replenished, but the outflows reflect sustained investor caution.
   Why it matters: The outflow volume underscores the heist's market impact and may influence exchange security standards going forward.
   https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/02/fbi-pins-63b-binance-crypto-heist-on-north-korean-hackers/5306889

3. *Tether invests $9M in AIsa AI agent infrastructure*
   Tether and HIVEN led a seed extension round for AIsa, a San Francisco AI agent infrastructure startup building a "Trusted Resource and Transaction Network" for enterprise AI agents. Total funding exceeds $9M, with strategic backers including Alibaba, Tribe Capital, and Saison Capital.
   Why it matters: Tether's backing signals enterprise validation of the AI agent infrastructure stack and could accelerate adoption of paid agent-to-agent payment systems.
   https://killtheai.com/news/top-10-ai-news-today-november-3-2026/

   Why it matters: The notifications validate long-standing concerns about commercial spyware proliferation and may prompt renewed policy scrutiny of surveillance tool exports.

5. *NYT and outlets sue Brave over AI content scraping*
   The New York Times, Condé Nast, and other major publishers have filed suit against Brave Browser alleging it scraped copyrighted content and "unlawfully distributing it to its own customers, including Anthropic and Perplexity." The case could establish new precedent for AI training data rights and publisher compensation.
   Why it matters: The litigation directly targets the business model of AI aggregators and may force restructuring of how large language models are trained on web content.
